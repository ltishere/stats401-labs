Exploring the DKU Undergraduate Bulletin

Formal document structure vs. semantic structure · STATS 401 Lab 8

1. Bulletin / Corpus Description

Source: Bulletin of Duke Kunshan University Undergraduate Instruction, 2021–2022 edition (V2021-22), 400 pages. Official DKU PDF: link. Accessed: 2026.9.22.

Processing: Text was extracted with PyMuPDF. Chapter, section and subsection labels come from the PDF's built-in bookmarks (5 levels, 1,142 entries); each text block was assigned to the nearest preceding heading on the page. Paragraphs split across pages were merged, standalone page numbers and heading lines were removed, passages over 220 words were split at sentence boundaries, and passages under 12 words (course codes, credit lines, table cells, calendar fragments) and exact duplicates were dropped.

5. Semantic Topics and Method

Embeddings. Each passage was encoded with the sentence-transformers model all-MiniLM-L6-v2 into a 384-dimensional vector and L2-normalized, so cosine similarity equals the dot product. Text was only whitespace-normalized before embedding (no stop-word removal or lemmatization).

Projection. UMAP (n_components=2, n_neighbors=15, min_dist=0.15, metric="cosine", random_state=401) reduces the embeddings to 2D for display only.

Clustering. K-means (random_state=401, n_init=10) was run on the original 384-dimensional embeddings, not on the UMAP coordinates. K was chosen by comparing cosine silhouette scores for K = 6–16 (all between 0.085 and 0.107, highest at K = 15) together with manual inspection: K = 12 left registration, study-away and student-service passages merged with unrelated content, while K = 15 separated them into interpretable policy topics.

Labeling. Each topic was labeled by reading the four passages closest to its centroid alongside its top class-based TF-IDF terms (unigrams and bigrams, English stop words plus corpus-wide words such as "students" and "course" removed).

Neighbors and typicality. The five nearest neighbors of each passage are computed by cosine similarity in the full 384-dimensional space. "Section typicality" is the cosine similarity between a passage and the mean embedding of its formal section.

6. Semantic Findings

1. What are the major semantic topics?
The 15 topics fall into two families. Nine are disciplinary and come almost entirely from Part 10 (majors and course descriptions): Physical Education & Sports; Biology & Chemistry; Language Learning; Math, Computer Science & Data Science; Society, Culture & Global Humanities; Politics, Economics & Behavior; Environment & Global Health; Media & Arts; and Asia Studies. Six are institutional: Fees, Aid & Student Services; Grading & Placement Credit; Degree Credits, Matriculation & Transfer; Registration, Leave & Academic Calendar; Major Requirement Lists & Electives; and Interdisciplinary Seminars & Advising. Because course descriptions make up 651 of the 1,157 passages, disciplinary topics occupy most of the map, while policy topics form a separate region.

2. Which topics appear across multiple formal sections?
Degree Credits, Matriculation & Transfer is the most widely distributed topic: it appears in 21 of the 25 matrix rows, from General Information and The Curriculum to Study Away and Useful Contacts. Registration, Leave & Academic Calendar appears in 14 rows and Fees, Aid & Student Services in 12, spanning Parts 4–9. Credit and enrollment rules are therefore not confined to the Academic Procedures chapter but are restated throughout the bulletin. By contrast, the disciplinary topics are concentrated in the two Part 10 rows.

3. Which formal sections contain the most semantically diverse content?
Measured by the normalized entropy of each section's topic distribution, Course Descriptions (14 of 15 topics, entropy 0.89) and Majors (11 topics, 0.81) are the most diverse, as expected for sections that describe every discipline. Among policy sections, The Curriculum (6 topics, 0.57), Student Affairs and Campus Life (0.56) and Admission, Scholarships and Financial Aid (0.55) are the most mixed. The least diverse are Transfer of Work Taken Elsewhere and the Academic Calendar, which each fall entirely into a single topic.

4. Which passages from different sections are semantically similar?
The nearest-neighbor view reveals repeated policy text. The rule that students may receive at most 40 credits taken elsewhere appears both in Transfer of Work Taken Elsewhere (Part 6) and in Career Services, Study Away, and Research Opportunities (Part 8), with a cosine similarity of 0.92. A passage on medical course withdrawal (Undergraduate Course Load) closely matches one on medical leave of absence (Changes in Status, 0.90), and the disclaimer that not every listed course is offered every term is repeated in both Majors and Course Descriptions (0.89). These pairs show where the same concept is split across the bulletin's formal structure.

6. Where do "credit", "graduation", "registration" and "academic integrity" occur?
"Credit" is the most scattered concept: 164 passages across 12 topics and 18 sections, mainly Grading & Placement Credit, Major Requirement Lists, and Degree Credits & Transfer, so it forms several separate regions on the map rather than one. "Graduation" appears in 32 passages across 5 topics and 12 sections. "Registration" is much more concentrated: 9 passages in only 2 topics, mostly Registration, Leave & Academic Calendar. The exact phrase "academic integrity" appears in only 3 passages, and the Academic Integrity section itself (5 passages) is split across four topics, so the concept never forms its own semantic cluster.

7. Design Description

Embeddings and projection. Each of the 1,157 passages was encoded with the all-MiniLM-L6-v2 sentence-transformer into a normalized 384-dimensional vector. UMAP (cosine metric, 15 neighbors) projects these vectors to 2D for display only; clustering, nearest neighbors and section typicality are all computed in the full embedding space, so the map is an approximate picture of semantic relationships rather than their source.

Topics. K-means with K = 15 was chosen by comparing silhouette scores and inspecting the clusters by hand. Each topic was labeled after reading the passages closest to its centroid together with its top class-based TF-IDF terms.

Encodings. On the map, position shows semantic similarity, hue shows topic (a categorical variable), and point area shows passage length. In the matrix, rows follow the bulletin's own order, and cell color encodes each topic's share of a section rather than raw counts, so small policy sections stay readable next to the 651-passage Course Descriptions row; exact counts are printed in the cells.

Coordination. All controls write to one shared state, so search, section and topic filters combine instead of overwriting each other. Clicking a matrix cell highlights its passages on the map, selecting a point outlines its cell in the matrix, and search results are summarized in the matrix as matching counts per cell. The detail panel lists each passage's five nearest neighbors and flags those from a different section, which is how repeated rules such as the 40-credit transfer cap were found.

Findings. Together, the views show that the bulletin's formal structure separates academic disciplines cleanly but scatters credit and enrollment rules across many chapters.
