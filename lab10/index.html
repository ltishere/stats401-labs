Camera and microphone start only after you click. Video frames are processed locally in your browser (MediaPipe) and never uploaded. Speech recognition uses the browser's Web Speech API (Chrome/Edge), which sends audio to the browser vendor's speech service.

How to Use

Voice. Click Start Voice Control and allow the microphone (Chrome or Edge). Voice stays on until you click the button again. Say a command such as “day 42”, “next”, “show Europe” or “highlight Ion Systems”. The panel shows what was heard and which action ran; unrecognized phrases show a hint instead of doing anything.

Gesture. Click Start Camera and allow the webcam; the hand model loads once (a few seconds). Keep one hand in view. Swipe left/right to step through days; hold an open palm to play, a fist to pause, and a victory sign to reset. Each gesture has a cooldown so one motion triggers one action.

Fallback. Buttons, the slider and the Focus dropdowns do everything the novel inputs do. Keyboard: Space play/pause, ←/→ previous/next day, Esc clear focus. Hover nodes and links for tooltips.

Design Reflection

I extended my Lab 7 animated commercial-network visualization with two novel modalities: voice commands and webcam hand gestures. The core task in Lab 7 is temporal navigation — stepping through 60 days and stopping on a moment to see who trades with whom — while keeping attention on the network. With a mouse, every step means looking away to the slider. Swiping left or right maps naturally to previous/next day because time already runs left to right, and an open palm or fist mirrors “go” and “stop.” Voice supports a second task: jumping directly to a day (“day 42”) or focusing on one region or company (“show Europe”, “highlight Ion Systems”), which otherwise requires scrubbing or searching a dropdown. Both modalities call the same functions as the buttons, so mouse and keyboard remain full fallbacks. Limitations: in testing, gesture recognition was the weak point. Static gestures were often not detected, and a small hand movement interrupted recognition; swipes were frequently missed when too fast or too slow. I relaxed the stillness and timing thresholds, but gestures remain less reliable than voice or buttons.
