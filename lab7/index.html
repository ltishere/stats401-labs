Temporal Network Questions

1. How does overall network connectivity change across the 60 days?
Connectivity is stable for the first 40 days at about 5.5 active links per day, then rises sharply in the final third to 7.1 links per day in days 41–50 and 7.4 in days 51–60. Daily transaction value grows even faster, from roughly $77,000 per day in days 1–20 to about $148,000 in days 51–60. The network therefore does not simply add links: existing and new relationships also carry much larger amounts, which is visible as both denser edges and consistently larger nodes late in the animation.

2. Which companies become more central or commercially active over time?
Comparing total volume in days 1–20 with days 41–60, Ion Systems grows the most (about $263,000 to $1,097,000) followed by Cedar Retail (about $252,000 to $902,000). Both more than quadruple and become the largest nodes in the later frames. Apex Textiles and Fusion Electronics also grow substantially. BlueRiver Logistics is the only company that does not grow (about $325,000 to $323,000), so its relative position in the network declines even though its own activity is unchanged.

3. Are there periods when the network consists of relatively separate clusters?
Yes. With only twelve companies and around five active links per day, almost every day up to day 41 breaks into two or three disconnected groups rather than one connected component. Day 1 is typical: six active companies form three isolated pairs with no path between them. A few days are exceptions — on days 4 and 16 the active companies form a single connected cluster — but these are rare in the early period. After day 41 the higher link count makes a single connected component much more common, and fragmentation largely disappears.

4. Which commercial relationships appear, disappear, or become stronger over time?
Several relationships recur throughout the 60 days and visibly thicken. Ion Systems repeatedly trades with Fusion Electronics and Juniper Stores; Fusion Electronics also maintains recurring ties with Granite Wholesale and Delta Components; and Cedar Retail trades regularly with Evergreen Foods and BlueRiver Logistics. In each case the edges reappear across many frames and their stroke width grows, indicating rising transaction amounts rather than merely more frequent contact. Because link width encodes amount, these strengthening relationships are easy to follow, while the orange flash on entry makes newly formed links stand out and the fade-out marks relationships that lapse on a given day.

5. Does the network become more or less cross-regional over time?
The network is already strongly cross-regional and becomes more so. Cross-region links make up 62–65% of activity through the first 40 days, then rise to 73% in days 41–50 and 72% in days 51–60. Dashed edges dominate the later frames, indicating that the growth in connectivity comes mainly from new ties between Asia, North America and Europe rather than from denser within-region trade.
