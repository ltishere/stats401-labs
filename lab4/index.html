Lab 4: Cleaning Web Data for Visualization

Sentiment of 3,000 tweets from the 20 most-followed accounts on Twitter, estimated with a RoBERTa model fine-tuned on social-media text.

Analysis

The dataset contains 52,542 tweets from the 20 most-followed Twitter accounts, with author, timestamp, language, likes and retweets. Cleaning (clean_tweets.py): the id column was stored in scientific notation and had already lost precision, so duplicates were removed on author, timestamp and text instead; latitude, longitude and country were over 99% empty and dropped; timestamps were day-first, so the format was forced rather than inferred; HTML entities were unescaped and 5,608 non-English tweets removed, since the model is English-only. From the remaining 46,934 tweets, 3,000 were sampled (seed 42). Sentiment came from cardiffnlp/twitter-roberta-base-sentiment-latest, keeping all three class probabilities and defining sentiment_score = P(positive) − P(negative). These are model-generated estimates, not ground-truth labels. The chart encodes account vertically and the share of each sentiment class horizontally, ordered by mean score. Two patterns: sentiment is skewed positive almost everywhere (1,855 positive against 175 negative), matching accounts built on promotion and thanks; and @cnnbrk is the sole exception at −0.33, the only account whose tweets are more often negative than positive, since breaking-news headlines report disasters in flat language.
