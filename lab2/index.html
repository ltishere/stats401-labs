Lab 2: Multivariate Visualization with D3

Twelve cities described by four variables: population, average temperature, development level, and region. Hover over a bar to see the exact values for that city.

Design Justification

Variable Type Visual channel
population Ratio Bar length
temp_c Interval Bar colour (blue to red)
development_level Ordinal Bar thickness
region Nominal Grouping into panels

I chose a grouped bar chart rather than a scatterplot because only one of the four variables is a true ratio quantity. Population is ratio-scaled, so it has a meaningful zero and can be read as a length starting from the origin; length is the channel people compare most accurately, which makes population the primary message of the chart. Average temperature is interval data, where differences matter but zero is arbitrary, so a diverging blue-to-red colour ramp fits it well: readers judge relative warmth without being asked to treat 0 °C as "nothing". Development level is ordinal with only three steps, so I mapped it to bar thickness, a channel that carries an obvious order (thin → thick) but not precise numeric meaning. Region is nominal and unordered, so instead of a colour scheme that would compete with temperature, I split the chart into four labelled panels; grouping shows category membership without implying any ranking.

I hope viewers notice that the largest and coolest cities cluster in the West and North, that warm cities in the South are small and mostly low development, and that within every region thicker bars tend to be the longer ones, suggesting population and development level move together. City names and tooltips let individual cities be identified.
